The challenge

Many hydration apps can make a simple routine feel like a chore: they may ask for an account up front, rely on rigid targets, or provide little encouragement. Hydration Buddy explores a lighter approach: let someone open the tracker and start logging, while keeping goals flexible and health guidance cautious.

Product principle: support a useful habit without presenting a generic number as a personal medical instruction.

My role

I shaped the product direction through iterative decisions about the experience, features, accessibility, privacy, and health boundaries. I used AI-assisted development to build and refine the working app, then reviewed the published experience and its limitations.

The project is a practical example of turning a product idea into a usable, publicly available web app and making trade-offs explicit as it grew.

Key product decisions

Start without signup

People can use the tracker right away. Drink logs, goals, and preferences stay in the current browser, avoiding account friction and a cloud database for a first version.

Keep goals under the user's control

A general guide is clearly labeled, and users can enter a target based on their care team's advice. Weather prompts do not override a personal goal.

Make the habit feel friendly

Chat, optional spoken replies, configurable reminders, and a celebration at goal completion offer encouragement while keeping interaction optional.

Design for everyday access

Responsive layouts, clear labels, keyboard focus styles, reduced-motion support, and an installable app shell help the experience work across devices and preferences.

Trade-offs and learning

  • Privacy vs. continuity: local storage keeps setup simple and private, but the log does not sync across devices and can be lost when browser data is cleared.
  • Reminders vs. reliability: browser timers are straightforward, but they pause when the page is closed. The interface explains this limitation.
  • Personalization vs. medical boundaries: weather can add context, but it cannot determine an individual's fluid needs. The app keeps care-team instructions in control.

What I would validate next

This version is a working product prototype. The next step is to learn from real use before adding complexity.

Run a small usability review

Ask a few people to complete key tasks: log a drink, change a goal, start a reminder, and find the privacy information. Record friction and improve the flow.

Measure adoption with consent

If usage analytics are ever added, explain them clearly and collect only what is needed. For now, no analytics are used and no usage claims are made.
